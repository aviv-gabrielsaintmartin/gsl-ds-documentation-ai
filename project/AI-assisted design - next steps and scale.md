**AI cuts time on four steps:**

- The PM's first-idea prototype.
- The designer's benchmark.
- The designer's production and variants.
- The handoff between designer and dev.

---

## 1. Where we want to go

**Save time between the idea and the decision.**

- Align earlier, and prototype faster.
- Time saved on making screens goes into exploring, testing and delivery prep.
- Everything rests on the GSL Design System.

**Pilot target:** a functional prototype in 1–3 days, instead of 5–10.

**Better alternatives, not more of them.**

- Each exploration shows the competitors and best practices it drew on.
- A number of alternatives would not judge their quality.

**Time saved on four steps:** first-idea prototype, benchmark, production, handoff.

- No baseline exists yet, so today's times get measured first.
- These are targets to measure, not commitments.
- We count learning and time saved, never screens generated.

**Every prototype has a level:** exploration, test or delivery prep.
Speed must never blur prototyping and validating.

## 3. The flow

### Today (to validate)

1. **Idea and PRD** — PM
2. **HiFi mocks and variants** — Designer
3. **Prototype** — Designer
4. **Decision** — PM, designer and dev
5. **Handoff** — Designer to dev
6. **Front-end code** — Dev
7. **Design QA** — Designer and dev

Steps 6 to 8 loop back and forth.

### With AI

1. **Idea and PRD** — PM and designer together.
2. **Kickoff** — The PRD becomes a real screen. PM, designer and dev discuss something concrete from day one. *Saves PM time. Proved on iOS, still slow.*
3. **Exploration and benchmark** — Designer with AI, in a design tool. Several realistic directions, built from design system components and quality benchmarks. *Saves benchmark time. Not tested.*
4. **Refinement** — Designer. Finalises the chosen direction.
5. **Full-flow prototype** — Designer. Solid enough for user tests. *Target: end of 2026.*
6. **Variants, states and edge cases, before the decision** — AI produces and reveals them, designer and dev validate. Errors, empty states, responsive versions, copy, exceptions. *Saves production and handoff time. Not tested.*
7. **Decision** — PM, designer and dev, on fuller information.
8. **Front-end code** — Dev, assisted, built on the design system. *Not tested. Owned by the tech squad.*
9. **QA** — Automatic check of design-code gaps. Designer and dev handle only critical gaps. *Not tested. Owned by the tech squad.*

AI changes every step. Steps 3 and 4 replace wireframes and mocks. Variants and edge cases move before the decision.

**A prototype is throwaway.**

- It is built in app code or in a design-first tool.
- Its code is not always usable in production.
- A dev still writes the production code.

## 4. Four use cases

### Align at kickoff — for PMs

**A PM turns a PRD into a working screen to align the team.**

- PM, designer and dev discuss something real from day one.
- Status: proved on iOS.
- Next step: open it to the PM AI guild.
- Needs: a tech squad to speed up generation.
- Deadline: to set.

### Explore alternatives — for designers

**A designer turns hypotheses into several realistic options.**

- Options use official components, main states and plausible data.
- Options draw on competitor references and best practices.
- Status: not tested. The tool produces one version today.
- Next step: a designer defines what ideation needs.
  - Which tools to use.
  - Whether the design system rules allow several options.
- Needs: one designer dedicated to the topic.
- Deadline: to set.

### Prototype the full flow — for designers

**A designer turns a main screen into a full, testable journey.**

- The prototype must be solid enough for user tests.
- Status: not tested. Every run so far built one screen.
- Next step: test it on a real subject.
- Needs: one designer.
- Deadline: tested and validated by 31 December 2026.

### Reveal edge cases — for designers and devs

**AI lists errors, empty states and dependencies before delivery.**

- Designer and dev agree on them before development starts.
- Status: not tested.
- Next step: define the method with a designer.
- Needs: one dedicated dev, or a team.
- Deadline: to set.

## 5. Prototype levels

**A prototype shows an idea. It does not validate it on its own.**

| Level | Purpose | Who decides | Use cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Exploration** | Show an idea, align the team | PM alone | Align at kickoff |
| **Test** | Test a hypothesis or a journey | PM and designer | Explore alternatives, full flow, reveal edge cases |
| **Delivery prep** | Define what gets built | Design and tech review | Front-end code and QA, after the decision |

- An exploration prototype never claims the concept is validated.
- Levels come from the design AI strategy brief.

## 6. How it scales

**Every PM and designer gets the tool, with weekly time to use it.**

- Install path: owned by the tech squad.
- Weekly AI time: agreed with managers.
- Ambassadors: the PM AI guild and the design AI guild.
- Guilds collect feedback and share what works.

---

## 7. Risk: one person becomes the bottleneck

**Today I build, fix and document alone. That cannot scale.**

- My role becomes organising: sort feedback, assign it, review it.
- Others contribute by asking questions and by doing.
- Example: a designer writes the missing spacing rules.
- Each use case has its own owner.
- A short contribution guide is needed before the guilds start.

---

## 8. What I need

- **A tech squad:** faster generation, and Android building from a spec.
- **One designer** dedicated to exploring alternatives.
- **One designer** for the full flow, through December.
- **One dev, or a team,** for edge cases.
- **Guild time** that covers contributing as well as using.
